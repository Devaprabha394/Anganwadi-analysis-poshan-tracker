# 📊 Analysis of Anganwadi Centres and Beneficiary Coverage Using Poshan Tracker Data

## 1. Project Overview

This project presents a comprehensive data analytics study of Anganwadi Centres across India using the Poshan Tracker dataset published by the Ministry of Women and Child Development (MoWCD) through the India Data Portal. The project follows the complete data analytics lifecycle, including data collection, data understanding, data cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory data analysis (EDA), and data visualization.

The analysis evaluates infrastructure, workforce availability, beneficiary coverage, nutrition service delivery, and digital governance indicators to identify operational patterns and regional disparities across states. The findings are translated into strategic recommendations and an implementation framework to support evidence-based policymaking and strengthen Anganwadi service delivery.

---

# 2. Project Aim

To analyze the operational performance of Anganwadi Centres across India using Poshan Tracker data and generate meaningful insights that support evidence-based decision-making for improving maternal and child welfare services.

---

# 3. Project Objectives

- Understand the structure and quality of the Poshan Tracker dataset.
- Perform comprehensive data cleaning and preprocessing.
- Conduct exploratory data analysis to identify meaningful trends and relationships.
- Evaluate infrastructure, beneficiary coverage, nutrition services, workforce availability, and digital governance indicators.
- Compare programme performance across different states and centre categories.
- Generate strategic recommendations and policy implications based on analytical findings.

# 7. Project Flow (Steps Used)

### Step 1 – Dataset Collection

Collected the Poshan Tracker dataset from the India Data Portal, published by the Ministry of Women and Child Development (MoWCD). The dataset contains nationwide information on Anganwadi Centres, beneficiary coverage, infrastructure, workforce, nutrition services, and digital governance indicators.

### Step 2 – Importing Required Libraries

Imported essential Python libraries, including Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Plotly, to support data manipulation, statistical analysis, and visualization.

### Step 3 – Dataset Loading and Initial Exploration

Loaded the dataset into Jupyter Notebook and examined its structure, dimensions, column names, data types, and descriptive statistics to gain an initial understanding of the data.

### Step 4 – Data Understanding

Analyzed dataset attributes, numerical and categorical variables, summary statistics, and category distributions to understand the characteristics of the available information before preprocessing.

### Step 5 – Data Cleaning and Preprocessing

Prepared the dataset by performing missing value analysis, null value treatment, duplicate removal, data type correction, date format standardization, column renaming, consistency checking, and outlier detection to ensure data quality and reliability.

### Step 6 – Feature Engineering

Created additional analytical features, including **Total Anganwadi Staff** and **Beneficiary per Staff Ratio**, to better evaluate workforce capacity and operational efficiency.

### Step 7 – Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Conducted comprehensive univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analyses to answer research questions related to infrastructure, beneficiary coverage, nutrition service delivery, workforce availability, digital governance, and state-wise programme performance.

### Step 8 – Data Visualization

Developed a variety of visualizations, including bar charts, histograms, scatter plots, box plots, pie charts, and heatmaps, to effectively communicate trends and analytical findings.

### Step 9 – Insight Generation

Interpreted analytical results to identify regional disparities, operational strengths, service delivery gaps, workforce patterns, beneficiary trends, and relationships among key programme indicators.

### Step 10 – Strategic Recommendations

Developed practical recommendations for improving infrastructure, workforce planning, nutrition service delivery, digital governance, beneficiary coverage, and resource allocation based on the analytical findings.

### Step 11 – Implementation Framework

Proposed an implementation roadmap outlining priorities, stakeholder responsibilities, monitoring mechanisms, and measurable milestones for strengthening Anganwadi programme performance.

### Step 12 – Conclusion

Summarized the overall findings and demonstrated how data analytics can support evidence-based policymaking for improving maternal and child welfare services through stronger Anganwadi programmes.

# 10. Key Findings

- Significant state-wise variations exist in the number of Anganwadi Centres and beneficiary coverage across India.
- Workforce availability has a direct influence on the operational efficiency and service delivery capacity of Anganwadi Centres.
- Rural, Urban, Tribal, and JANMAN Anganwadi Centres exhibit distinct patterns in infrastructure availability, beneficiary coverage, and programme implementation.
- States with stronger infrastructure generally demonstrate better beneficiary outreach and service delivery performance.
- Nutrition service indicators, including Take Home Ration (THR) and Hot Cooked Meal (HCM) distribution, vary considerably across states, highlighting differences in programme execution.
- Digital governance indicators, such as Aadhaar verification and Health ID generation, show uneven adoption across different regions.
- Feature engineering revealed variations in beneficiary-to-staff ratios, suggesting unequal workload distribution among Anganwadi workers.
- Exploratory Data Analysis identified significant regional disparities in operational capacity, workforce strength, infrastructure, and service delivery.
- Multiple programme indicators collectively influence the overall effectiveness of Anganwadi Centres rather than any single factor.
- The findings emphasize the importance of data-driven planning, continuous monitoring, and targeted policy interventions to strengthen maternal and child welfare programmes.
